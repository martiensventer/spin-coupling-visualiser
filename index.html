Reading it. Each sphere has physical radius = the spin length (k⁄2); the colour on its surface is the local norm-squared |ψ(x)|², deepest at the peak and normalised to each state's own maximum. In the multi-view, every coupled sphere's colour is scaled by its channel amplitude λ(c) = ‖Pc(ψ̂v⊗ψ̂w)‖, so pale spheres are weakly populated channels. The dot (⬤) marks where the coupled norm peaks; in the focused view the construction reproduces it — start at v+w and run parallel to the bisector of v,w out to the radius-c⁄2 sphere (the chevrons mark the two lines as parallel). Switch on Full for the complete construction — both parallelograms, the law-of-sines triangle, and the angles γ⁄2, α−γ⁄2. In the Cross-section view, Construction draws that same parallel line through all the nested shells, so you can see the peaks of every channel lined up on it.
Conventions & what's computed

A state in Vk is the spin j=k⁄2 representation; its classical vector has length k⁄2. Coupled labels follow the fusion rule c ∈ {|a−b|, |a−b|+2, …, a+b}. Only the relative angle γ between v and w affects the coupled shapes and coefficients (everything else is an overall rotation), so γ is the single physical input beyond the two spins.

ψp(k) = ck(p̄₀Z + p̄₁W)k  ·  density |ψ(x)|² = |⟨ψpₓ|ψ⟩|² (Husimi / overlap)
coupled: ψ(c)v;w ∝ Pc(ψv⊗ψw), β(c)M = Σ ⟨j₁m₁ j₂m₂|Jc M⟩ ψv,m₁ ψw,m₂
peak angle from v: α = γ⁄2 + arcsin( (b−a)/c · sin(γ⁄2) )

The coupled state's full density is computed exactly from the coupled coefficient vector, so the ⬤ peak is the true argmax — it lands on the point given by the α formula as a check, not an assumption. The coefficient uses the orthogonal-projection (channel-amplitude) normalisation you chose, so Σc (λ(c))² = 1. For every γ<180° the norm peaks at the single point m; only as v,w approach antiparallel does m degenerate into a ring (of height |a−b|), which is then drawn directly on the sphere.
